# CleanJibe — what Ask CleanJibe knows

GENERATED by web/tools/make_assistant_knowledge.py from docs/copy. Do not edit.

CleanJibe analyses wingfoil sessions: when you were up on the foil, every flight, and a verdict on every turn (flew through, touchdown or fell in, and clean). It runs as an iPhone app, in the browser at cleanjibe.org, and as a Garmin watch app that judges live on the wrist. It is free and non-commercial, built by riders.

Answering rules: use only facts from this file and from the state you are given. Never estimate a number yourself. A number marked estimated stays estimated. If the answer is not here, say you do not know and point to cleanjibe.org/beta/#tell. Answer in the language of the question. Keep the rider vocabulary below exactly. A session's speeds come in knots (the fields ending in Kn); say every speed in the rider's unit from units.speed, converted (1 kn = 1.852 km/h = 1.151 mph = 0.514 m/s), and never give the knots beside it unless asked. The help's thresholds are written in km/h; convert those to the rider's unit too. The state names no place: never say where a session was ridden. The example session below (Lake Garda) is the app's demo, never the rider's. There is no Android app; on Android, CleanJibe is the browser app at cleanjibe.org/app. The vocabulary rule holds in every language: never "success", "successful" or "carried", nor their translations (German "erfolgreich", "Erfolg"); say flew through, clean, dry, touchdown or fell in.

## How CleanJibe talks (docs/voice.md)

1. **One thought per sentence.** About 12 words; never more than 20. Two "and"s or a pair of
   dashes is two sentences.
2. **The rider first, the mechanics after.** *Your session shows up in CleanJibe by
   itself.* Then, where it helps: *intervals.icu carries it over, because Garmin has no open
   API.* A sentence about the mechanics is allowed when a rider can do something with it:
   choose a tool, fix a setup, understand a verdict. It is cut when it only describes what
   the software did.
3. **What, then why — and the why only if it changes what the rider does.** "Restart the
   watch once after installing." needs no reason. "Sessions arrive by themselves" needs one
   clause: "Garmin has no open API, so intervals.icu is the bridge."
4. **No em-dash, no semicolon, no parenthesis in rider text.** A dash is a second sentence
   hiding. A parenthesis is either needed (then a sentence) or not (then gone).
5. **No framing, no pointe, no fragment.** Banned shapes: *the one thing…*, *the half only
   you can do*, *exactly as…*, *which is the whole point*, *not X, but Y* as a flourish, *and
   that is why…*, *…, nothing else.*, and a verbless fragment as a sentence (*Free.* *Once.*
   *Once.*). A fragment is allowed as a heading or as the tagline under the wordmark only; the tagline is *Your WingFoil session, measured.* (Jan, 23 September 2026). Say the thing.
6. **Rider vocabulary, exactly.** *flew through*, *touchdown*, *fell in*, *clean*, *dry
   streak*, *on foil*. Never *carried*, *success*, *no-fall*, *swim rate*
   (`docs/copy/phrases.json` → `lexicon`). Path notation for the app: *Settings → intervals.icu*.
7. **Second person, present tense.** *You*, not *the rider*; *opens*, not *will open*. The app
   is *CleanJibe* or *the app*. *We* is the team that builds it, used sparingly and where a
   person is meant: *tell us what you saw*, *we read every mail*. Never *I*.
8. **Numbers stay numbers.** *2 s*, *500 m*, *10 riders*, *42 watches*. No "a handful", no
   "about ten" where the number is known.
9. **Register 2 gets one line of energy, not a paragraph.** A question, an image, a promise
   — then back to plain. Excitement is a single sentence that earns the next tap.
10. **Every cut fact keeps a home.** If a sentence goes, its fact goes to `docs/`, the help,
    or a store text, or it was not a fact.

11. **Wingfoiler words yes, IT words no.** A wingfoiler's insider word is welcome and is
    taught once in the glossary: *estimated record*, *alpha 500*, *dry streak*, *foil up*.
    An IT word never reaches a rider: *route*, *door*, *class (b)*, *pipeline*, *re-derive*,
    *digest*, *payload* stay in `docs/`. When in doubt: would another wingfoiler use the
    word on the beach?

## What the numbers mean (the glossary)

- **On foil**: How much of your session you spent flying rather than just moving. Also printed as: Foil time, foil, foil time.
- **Flights & touchdowns**: One takeoff starts a flight. A touchdown or a fall ends it. Both are counted. Also printed as: Flights, Longest flight, flights, longest flight.
- **Turn verdicts**: Every turn gets one: flew through, touchdown, or fell in. Also printed as: flew · touchdown · fell in.
- **Dry streak**: How many jibes in a row you stayed out of the water, and the best run of the day. Also printed as: best streaks.
- **CPH**: Clean jibes per hour. Clean: flew through, held your speed, and 10 quiet seconds after. Also printed as: CPH · clean jibes per hour.
- **JPH**: Dry jibes per hour. Falling in more often cannot raise it. Also printed as: JPH · dry jibes per hour.
- **TPH**: Dry turns per hour, tacks and jibes together. It takes the place of JPH once a session has tacks in it. Also printed as: TPH · dry turns per hour.
- **falls / h**: How often you fell in, per hour. Every fall counts, in a turn or not. Also printed as: WPH.
- **Speed records**: Your fastest 2 seconds, 10 seconds, 500 m and nautical mile. The speedsurfing world uses the same windows. Also printed as: Best 1 NM, Best 10 s, Best 100 m, Best 2 s, Best 250 m, Best 500 m, avg speed, best 1 NM, best 10 s, best 100 m, best 2 s, best 250 m, best 500 m.
- **Best 5×10 s**: The mean of your best five separate 10-second runs. They may not overlap, so one lucky reach cannot carry it. Also printed as: 5×10 s.
- **Alpha 500**: Your fastest 500 m that ends within 50 m of where it started. It contains a jibe, so it measures the turn as well as the speed.
- **Flew through**: The turn kept the foil, from the sweep until you were flying again. Also printed as: flew.
- **Clean**: A jibe that flew through, held 70 % of its entry speed, and stayed quiet for 10 s. Also printed as: Clean jibes, clean jibes.
- **Speed kept**: The share of counted turns that held 70 % of their entry speed and never dropped off the foil.
- **Dry**: You did not fall in. A touchdown still counts as dry.
- **Touchdown**: Your board touched the water and you got going again without falling in. A stop under 5 s counts as a touchdown. Longer, and it counts as a fall. Also printed as: Touchdowns, touchdowns.
- **Fell in**: Every time you ended up in the water, in a turn or in a straight line. Also printed as: Falls, falls, fell.
- **Takeoffs**: How many times you got up on the foil. One takeoff starts every flight. Also printed as: Got up, Planing starts, got up.
- **Attempts**: Every pumping burst, whether you got up or not. Attempts are takeoffs plus failed attempts. Also printed as: Failed attempts, Takeoff attempts, failed attempts.

### Bring it in

#### Get set up with intervals.icu
Set it up once, in 4 steps and about 5 minutes.
Your Garmin sessions come in by themselves, your old ones too. The easiest way is intervals.icu, because Garmin has no open API. You can also open a .fit by hand, from Files, Mail or a Garmin export ZIP.
Nothing is uploaded, and nothing changes in either account. CleanJibe downloads the original recording of each watersport session and analyses it on your phone.
Polar, Suunto and COROS work this way too. Their own apps sync to intervals.icu, and CleanJibe syncs from there.
The file your watch left there decides how much you get. A FIT gives you the full analysis. A GPX, or a TCX without speed, gives estimated speed records.
- 1. Create a free intervals.icu account: Open intervals.icu and sign up. It is free, and the Google, Strava or e-mail account you already have will do.
- 2. Connect Garmin in intervals.icu: In intervals.icu, Settings → device connections: connect your Garmin account. Your history back-fills in a few minutes, and every new session arrives on its own.
- 3. Generate your personal API key: Still in intervals.icu: Settings → Developer Settings → API Key. Copy it. Developer settings are free for every user. No subscription is needed.
- 4. Paste the key into CleanJibe: In CleanJibe, open Settings → intervals.icu and paste the key. Tap Save & check. CleanJibe checks it and says how many activities it can see.
- Why Garmin says Windsurf: Garmin has no wingfoil profile. Garmin and Strava call the session Windsurf. CleanJibe calls it Wingfoil.
- Where Garmin Connect shows the jibes: Under Connect IQ on the activity page: foil time, flights, the best 2 seconds, jibes and tacks.
- When the jibes and tacks show up: They appear after a few minutes of flying, once the watch has worked out the wind. Set it by hand to have them from the start.
- Why the Runs card stays empty: Runs belong to Garmin's own windsurf profile. No Connect IQ app can fill that card. CleanJibe's flights are the runs.

#### Reading the map
What the chips, the colours, the arrows and the dots mean.
Every chip under the map is a switch. Tap one to hide that kind of mark on the map and in the speed chart at once.
A hidden chip stays in place, struck through, and "show all" brings everything back. A chip this session has nothing for cannot be switched. The three rows are the track, the marks on it, and the map's own controls.
Tap the track to move the replay playhead. Tap a mark, or a flown stretch, for its own facts.
In the iPhone app, one finger on the map scrolls the page. Use two fingers to move or zoom the map.
- The track: Tinted by phase: teal where you were flying, grey where you were not. Small chevrons along it point the way you were riding.
- The dots: Verdicts on one ladder: green flew through, orange touchdown, red fell in. Grey is a course change and no verdict at all.
- Solid or hollow: The fill tells two kinds of mark apart without a second colour. Solid is a turn's outcome. Hollow is a flight that ended on a straight line, outside any turn.
- A star: A clean jibe, in a green of its own, taking the place of that jibe's dot. It needs both its chips: hide the outcome and the star goes with it.
- Arrows, not dots: Takeoffs: an up-arrow got you up, a red u-turn is an attempt that did not. One chip hides both halves.
- Bands and drops: The indigo bands are pump bursts. The cyan drop is the barometer seeing your wrist go under.

#### Flights and foil time
How often you got up, how long you stayed up, and how far you went.
A flight is every stretch you stayed up on the foil. It starts once you hold 12 km/h for 2 s. It ends when you drop under 8 km/h for 3 s.
A flight is dated back to the first moment that counted, at both ends. Anything under 5 s is not a flight.
A touchdown or a fall ends a flight. The next takeoff starts a new one.
Taxiing, time in the water and the drift upwind count against your time on foil. A gap in the recording does not.
- Foil time and On foil: Foil time is the minutes you flew. On foil is their share of timer time, which leaves out any stretch where the recording stopped or lost GPS.
- Flights: How many separate times you got up and stayed up.
- Longest flight: Your longest flight by time, with its distance underneath. Many short flights and one long one can give the same On foil.
- Distance: How far you went, flying, taxiing and drifting. It adds up the watch's measured speed rather than positions, because position noise inflates a distance at low speed.

#### Jumps [only in: beta, dev]
How many times you jumped, and an estimate of the highest.
The motion sensor on your wrist feels each jump. It notices the pop off the water, the time in the air and the landing. A jump counts only on the foil, at speed and outside a turn.
The height is worked out from that motion, so it is always marked estimated. No jump, no card.
- Where it shows: On the Flights tab of a wingfoil session. It needs your wrist's motion: the CleanJibe Apple Watch app, or the Garmin app with Log wrist motion on.
- On the Garmin watch: The beta counts your jumps live. Each one buzzes and shows JUMP with your count.

#### Speed records
Your fastest 2 seconds, 10 seconds, 500 m and nautical mile. The speedsurfing world uses the same windows.
The set is fixed, so your numbers compare with anyone's. It holds the four windows below, 100 m, 250 m and your best hour.
Best 5×10 s and Alpha 500 are defined in What the numbers mean.
All of them use the watch's Doppler speed, measured to the exact edge of each window. So a watch that records once a second scores the same as one that records four times. No slow stretch is thrown away.
A window never spans a recording gap. Tap a record card to see where on the track and on the speed trace it happened.
A record counts as measured only when the recording holds the receiver's own speed channel. The rest are marked estimated.
- Best 2 s: Your peak speed, averaged over 2 seconds. A single-sample maximum is noise. 2 seconds is a real burst, and the number most riders compare.
- Best 10 s: The fastest 10-second run, a burst you had to hold. Luck and a single gust cannot carry it, so it usually sits 1 to 3 knots below your 2 s.
- Best 500 m: Your fastest half-kilometre, measured along the line you actually rode. A curved run still counts, and cutting the corner flatters nothing.
- Best 1 NM: Your fastest nautical mile, 1852 m. On most spots it needs more than one leg, so it measures how well you keep speed through your turns.
- Knots or km/h: Knots by default. Settings → Units switches every speed on the phone to km/h.
- The other three readers: The watch app has its own switch, under Garmin Connect → CleanJibe → Settings. Garmin Connect follows its own. Strava shows windsurf in knots.
- A friend's session: Sessions a friend sent you stay out of your records, trends and totals.
- "Estimated": A recording with positions but no speed channel has its speed worked out from them, which reads high. Every GPX is one, and some converted exports. It is marked everywhere.

#### Measured and estimated speed records
Choose whether a record from a track without measured speed counts.
A measured record comes off a recording that carries your watch's own Doppler speed. An estimated one is worked out from positions, which reads high.
Only measured keeps estimated records out of your all-time table, your trends and your cards. The session that set one still shows it, marked.
Prefer measured is the default. A measured record wins its row, and an estimated one fills a row no measured record has reached.
Include estimated counts every record and marks the estimated ones. Settings → Speed records is where you choose.

#### The session story [only in: beta, dev]
One line over your numbers, and a chip on each record you beat.
Over a session's key numbers, one line says what stood out. That can be your clean jibes, or a record you beat this month, this season or ever.
Each record you beat gets a chip on its cell. With nothing to celebrate, the line is the plain tally.

#### Tacks, jibes and course changes
What counts as a turn, and what is just a change of direction.
Every jibe and tack you carve counts as a turn. To count, it needs 60° of heading change inside 8 s, on the foil or within 3 s of it.
It must also carve a real arc. That means at least 12 m of path, at a radius of 6 m or more. Spinning around beside the board carves no arc.
What kind of turn it was depends on the wind axis. A tack or a jibe needs 90° of heading change and a crossing of the axis:
- Jibe: Your course crosses the wind axis through downwind.
- Tack: Your course crosses the wind axis through upwind.
- Bear-away / round-up: A course change under 90°, or one that never crosses the axis. It stays out of the tack and jibe tallies.
- Turn: A turn on a session where the wind axis was too uncertain to name it. It is still counted, just unnamed.
- Most of my turns are: Your track gives the wind axis as a line. When a session cannot say which end the wind blew from, your habit does.
- Changing it later [iPhone app only]: Sessions already in your library change only when you re-run the analysis, from Settings → Storage.

#### Turn outcomes: flew through, touchdown, fell in
What actually happened to the foil in the turn.
Every turn ends one of three ways: flew through, touchdown, or fell in. CleanJibe watches from the turn start until you are flying again.
Flying again means 2 s back above 70 % of your entry speed. The window closes after 12 s.
A jibe exited at marginal speed can bleed off for 6 to 12 seconds before the foil stalls. That mush-out is the jibe's fault. A jibe you power out of closes its window in a second or two.
Inside that window, your speed is always read. So is the barometer, because a wrist under water looks like a huge drop in altitude. On a CleanJibe watch recording, the accelerometer is read too.
- If you never get going again: The window follows you for up to 30 s instead of 12. The stop you end in is then this turn's.
- Flew through: You never left the foil. No sample in the window is off-foil.
- Touchdown: Your board touched the water and you got going again without falling in. A stop under 5 s counts as a touchdown. Longer, and it counts as a fall.
- Fell in: You stopped for more than 5 s, or the barometer says your wrist went under.
- Touchdowns on a straight line: A touchdown outside any turn is counted for the session. It ends the flight, like any touchdown.

#### Clean jibes
The jibe you were working for: foil up the whole way, speed held, straight back to riding.
This is the jibe you were working for. A **clean** jibe passes three gates: you flew through, held your speed, then 10 quiet seconds on the foil.
**Flew through** means you never lost the foil until you were flying again.
Holding the speed means your slowest point stays at or above 70 % of your entry speed. You never drop below the foil exit speed.
Staying quiet means the 10 s after the turn hold no touchdown or fall. They also hold no second or more off the foil, and no wrist under water.
Every clean jibe also flew through. A jibe that flew through can still miss clean, if you lost speed or touched down coming out.
- Jibes only: A tack has no clean reading to carry, so the Tacks card reports only how its tacks ended.
- Score: The share of your entry speed you held through the turn, 0 to 100. It is the evidence behind "clean", printed beside every turn.

#### Rates
Your jibes and falls per hour on the water, and when a session is too short for one.
A rate is a count per hour on the water. The hour is your timer time: the recording minus its pauses and gaps.
**CPH** counts your clean jibes, the jibe you were working for.
**JPH** counts your dry jibes: flew through or touchdown. Falling in more often never raises it.
Once a session has a tack, **TPH** takes its place and counts your dry turns.
**falls / h** counts every fall, in a turn or not.
Under 20 minutes on the timer, a session shows no rate. Five clean jibes in ten minutes would read as 30 an hour. Trends and records leave it out too.
The example session keeps its rates, so you can see them before your own.

#### Port / starboard
Which tack you were on going in, and which side you avoid.
The side is read from your wind angle before the turn. A 50/50 split means you work both sides equally. A lopsided split is the tack you quietly stop choosing, and usually the one worth practising.
The Trends screen plots this over time as "% port", with 50 % marked.

#### Fell in
Every time you ended up in the water, in a turn or in a straight line.
A fall means you stopped for more than 5 seconds, or the barometer caught your wrist going under.
The split matters. Falls in a turn are a turning problem. Falls in a straight line are a gust, a ventilation or a tip catching. Each fall is counted once. A fall inside a turn's window belongs to that turn.
The jibe tally is a different question. Its three counts are out of your jibes, so a fall in a straight line is not in them. This number counts the whole session.

#### Glide-outs
Flights that ended without ever stopping.
The flight ended and you kept moving. You settled onto the board and taxied on, or you chose to stop riding. No stop was ever measured, so this is not counted as a loss.
When a flight ends because the recording stopped, CleanJibe calls it unknown. There is nothing there to judge, so it stays out of every tally.

#### The Next session card [only in: beta, dev]
One thing to work on next time, from this session's turns.
The card sits at the top of the Turns tab. It names the tip that came up most often, and on how many of your turns.
It also compares your port and starboard entries and names the side to work on. It does that only with at least 3 turns on each side.

#### Attempts & getting up
How often you pumped, including the times you did not get up.
Attempts are takeoffs plus failed attempts. A pumping burst is a failed attempt when no flight starts within 10 seconds of your last stroke. Bursts closer together than that are chained into one attempt.
Takeoffs alone cannot show this. Getting up 20 times out of 22 looks like getting up 20 out of 40.
It needs the wrist accelerometer, which only the CleanJibe watch app records. Without it your failures are invisible, so the share is shown as unknown rather than a flattering 100 %.

#### Pumps to takeoff
How many strokes each flight cost you.
Every flight shows how many pump strokes it took to get up. The count starts at the rising speed before the flight, or at the pump burst that led into it.
Under 3 strokes is a free takeoff: the wind got you up alone. Free takeoffs say more about the conditions than about you. They stay out of the averages and are shown on their own.
When the recording cut a run short, its cost is unknown. The flight still counts as a takeoff that worked, but it stays out of the averages.

#### Pump strokes
Every stroke in the session, and the ones you did in flight.
Strokes are counted from the wrist accelerometer. Only the strength of each movement counts, so how your wrist was turned does not matter.
In-flight strokes hold or extend a glide rather than get you up. That is different work, so they are counted separately.

#### Heart rate: cost and coverage
How hard each takeoff hit you, and when to trust the number.
Heart-rate cost shows how hard each takeoff hit you. It is how far your pulse climbed from just before the pumping to its peak.
Your baseline is the median of the 10 s before the takeoff run. The peak is searched 30 s forward, because a wrist sensor trails effort by 10 to 20 s.
A negative cost means you were still recovering when you started. That is not the same as a takeoff that cost nothing.
The card does not appear when your recording has no usable heart rate. Nothing is estimated while the sensor was silent.
- Coverage: The share of a window with good readings, between 30 and 220 bpm and no more than 10 seconds apart. Below 60 % no number is shown.
- Why it drops out: A wrist sensor under a wetsuit sleeve in cold water drops out and sticks. Then you see no number rather than a guess.
- The fatigue chart: Your session in 20-minute blocks, each showing what its takeoffs cost, with the share of attempts that got up underneath. An empty block is shaded, not drawn as zero.
- Read the cost bars with the baseline note: A rise measured against a baseline that has drifted upward gets smaller as you tire. A shrinking late cost is not evidence that the takeoffs got easier.

#### Wind axis & confidence
The wind direction worked out from how you actually rode.
Your turns become tacks and jibes because CleanJibe knows the wind. It works the wind out from your own track, with no weather station.
It finds your two main reaching directions in the headings you flew. The wind axis is the line halfway between them.
That gives an axis but not a side. The no-go zone breaks the tie. Of the two ends, the wind came from the one you rode almost nothing within ±45° of.
Confidence combines how cleanly the two reaches separate with how decisive the no-go zone was. Below 50 % the axis is still shown, but your turns stay unnamed "turns" rather than tacks and jibes.
A wind direction you set on the watch always wins.

#### Windsurf (experimental) [only in: dev]
The same engine without the wing. The planing speeds are a guess.
A session can be analysed as **Wingfoil**, **Windsurf foil** or **Windsurf fin**. The row is on the session's Details tab, under "Analyse as". Changing it works that one session out again, and nothing else.
None of Garmin, Strava, intervals.icu and Apple Health has a wingfoil sport. Most riders record under the windsurf profile. A new session cannot say which rig it was ridden on.
So CleanJibe reads it as whatever you set under Settings → "I mostly ride". It is marked with a **?** until you have looked, and listed after each import. Sessions from the CleanJibe watch app are never asked about.
- What works: Jibes and tacks are the same detector, the same thresholds and the same three verdicts. So are the speed records, the wind axis, the wrist-under marks and the map.
- What is off: Pumping. With no wing to load, pump chips, stroke counts, pumps-to-takeoff and the heart-rate cost are **absent rather than zero**. A takeoff becomes a planing start.
- Windsurf foil: The wingfoil reading with pumping switched off. Every speed and every threshold is the same, because a foil flies the same way under either rig.
- Windsurf fin: Also moves the two speeds that decide when you are going. 20 km/h starts planing and 15 km/h stops it, against 12 and 8 on a foil.
- Those two speeds are provisional: They are a first guess, because we have no real fin sessions yet. If your planing time looks wrong, tell us that number.
- In the library: "Foil time" means planing time here, and "lost the foil" means stopped planing. Windsurf sessions count towards your trends. There is no separate record set.

### Something wrong?

#### When the sync does not work
The four things that actually go wrong, and the fix for each.
CleanJibe tells you what went wrong, because each problem has its own fix. A rejected key means your key. An empty list usually means Garmin is not connected yet. A network error is neither.
- "intervals.icu rejected the API key": The key is wrong, or you made a new one after pasting it. Copy it again from intervals.icu, Settings → Developer Settings. A stray space at either end breaks it.
- The sync succeeds but the list stays empty: Either Garmin is not connected in intervals.icu yet, or none of your activities is a watersport. CleanJibe takes Windsurf, Kitesurf, Sail, Surfing, SUP and anything named wing or foil.
- "Could not reach intervals.icu": The connection dropped, and your key is fine. Nothing was lost or half-imported. Check your connection and sync again.
- Older sessions are missing [iPhone app only]: The sync looks two years back and skips what the library already holds. For more, use Import → FIT or ZIP… with the Garmin export ZIP.

#### The watch update does not arrive
The store queued it without installing it. Restart the watch, then sync.
Garmin's Connect IQ store sometimes queues an update without installing it, most often on the fenix 5 Plus family.

If it still shows the old version, install it through Garmin Express over the cable.

#### When the watch and the phone show different numbers
This is normal, and the phone's number is the one to trust.
A session from the CleanJibe watch app brings the summary the watch worked out while you rode. The watch does that in one pass, with very little memory. The phone does the whole job again, and compares the two.

The phone's number is the one to go by. Nothing is wrong with your session. It only means the watch's live guess needs more work from us.
