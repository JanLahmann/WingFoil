Privacy

CleanJibe analyses your wingfoil sessions on the device you are holding. There is no CleanJibe account, no CleanJibe login, and no CleanJibe server that your sessions are sent to — because there is no CleanJibe server at all.

That is the whole policy. The rest of this page names every exception to it, because a privacy promise with an unnamed exception in it is not a promise.

The iPhone app — what stays on your phone

Everything, unless a line further down says otherwise.

The analysis runs on the phone

When you import a .fit or .gpx file — or a workout you recorded with Apple's Workout app — the app reads it and works out the flights, the turn verdicts, the speed records and the wind axis itself, on the device. Nothing is sent anywhere to be analysed, and there is nowhere it could be sent to.

Your library is a file in the app

Sessions live in a database and a file archive inside the app's own storage on your phone. No other app can read them. If you delete CleanJibe, they go with it.

There is no account

You are never asked for an email address, a password, a name or a phone number. CleanJibe has no user record for you anywhere, because it keeps no records anywhere.

The iPhone app — what leaves your phone

Nine entries, and not one of them is a surprise: an account you connected, something a map needs, the name of a place you sail, a mail you wrote and sent yourself, and — while this is a beta — Apple’s own crash reports and one small file the app reads to see whether your build is still the current one. There is no tenth.

intervals.icu — only if you connect it
If you paste your own intervals.icu API key into Settings, the app uses it to download your Garmin activities from intervals.icu so they arrive without you exporting files by hand. The key is stored in the iOS Keychain, is never copied into iCloud, and is sent only to intervals.icu, only as the login header on those requests. The traffic is one-way: CleanJibe reads your activity list and your original files, and never uploads a session, an analysis or anything about you. Clear the key in Settings and every request stops. Connect nothing and none is ever made.
Strava — only if you connect it
Import → Strava, or Settings → Strava, opens Strava's own consent page: you sign in to Strava there and not to us, and Strava itself tells you what is being asked for. What is asked for is read access (activity:read_all) and nothing else. CleanJibe lists your activities, and for the ones you pick it downloads their positions, their clock, their elevation and their heart rate. It never writes — no activity, no description, no comment — and nothing in the app is a quiet step towards it. The access token it gets back lives in the iOS Keychain on your phone, is never copied into iCloud, and is sent only to Strava. Disconnect, on either of those two screens, deletes it from the phone; to withdraw the permission at Strava's end as well, use strava.com/settings/apps, which is where any app's access is really revoked. Strava lets a new app connect a limited number of riders, which the app says out loud rather than reporting as a failure; if you are told the app is full, tell us. Sessions that arrive this way carry positions and no measured speed, so their speed records are marked uncertified — an accuracy note rather than a privacy one, and the Import screen makes it before you tap rather than after.
The Strava sign-in comes back through cleanjibe.org
Strava returns a rider only to an address on the one web domain it has on file for an app, and refuses custom app links outright. Ours is cleanjibe.org, so the last hop of the sign-in loads cleanjibe.org/strava/callback for about a frame and that page hands the result straight back to the app. It is a single static file with no stylesheet, no counter and nothing third-party on it — an authorization code is a credential for the next few seconds and must not be handed to anybody's script. GitHub Pages, which serves this site, sees that request the way it sees every other page load on it: a URL, which in this one case has that single-use code in it, a time and an IP address. There is no code of ours on that server and nothing is stored. The code is then exchanged for the token between your phone and Strava directly, and is worthless afterwards.
Apple Maps — when a map is on screen
The session map, the full-screen map, the replay and the share card's optional map background are all drawn by Apple's MapKit, which fetches map imagery for the area you sailed in. Apple therefore sees roughly where the session was, under Apple's own privacy policy and with no identifier of ours attached. The share card's map background is off unless you turn it on; the session maps you can simply not open.
Apple Maps again — the map this phone draws for a Garmin watch
A Garmin cannot be given a real map, so CleanJibe draws it one: a coarse land/water/road picture of a 3 km box around a spot, one or two kilobytes, which the watch blits under its breadcrumb. That picture is rendered by the same MapKit, which means Apple sees the area of that spot even with no map anywhere on your screen — the part worth saying plainly. It happens when you press Send map to watch, and by itself at app launch and after an import. Which spots: the two you have ridden most, unless you tick your own under Settings → Garmin watch → Map for the watch, which replaces that automatic pair with your picks. When nothing about the pair has changed since last time, nothing is rendered and nothing is asked of Apple. The finished picture goes over Bluetooth to the watch and to nobody else.
Apple's place-name lookup — one rounded coordinate, once per new spot
When the app meets a sailing spot it has not seen before, it asks Apple’s reverse-geocoding service what that place is called, so your spots read “Nago-Torbole” instead of “Spot 3”. What is sent is a single coordinate — the centre of that spot, rounded to about 110 m — and nothing else: no session, no track, no name, no identifier, no account, and no CleanJibe server anywhere in the path. Once per new spot, never again for a spot that already has a name, and never at all if you name your spots yourself. Apple sees roughly where you sail, under Apple’s own privacy policy.
A feedback mail — written by the app, sent by you
Menu → Support & ideas, the quiet Something off, or an idea? Send feedback at the foot of every page, and Report a problem with this session… on a session's share sheet all open one thing: a mail to info@cleanjibe.org in your own Mail app, with the facts already typed in. Those facts are the build number, your iPhone model, iOS version and locale, the paired watch and which version of the CleanJibe watch app it runs, and the shape of your library — how many sessions, and how many came in by each door. Sent from a session page it also names that session's date, spot, duration and source, and attaches that session's share card. There is no track, no file and no coordinate in it. Every line is editable, and nothing is sent until you tap Send: it is an ordinary mail, through your own mail account, to a mailbox. There is no CleanJibe server anywhere in that path either.
One session, sent by you, from its Share page
Send this session to the developer on a session’s Share page is in the public beta. It opens one mail with your notes on it and that session’s recording attached, as it was imported. The file holds your track, your heart rate and your times. It is used only to improve the detection and it is never published. Under your notes it carries the same facts the feedback mail carries, plus that session’s duration, distance, jibe tally and wind source. Nothing runs in the background and nothing is sampled: it happens once, for one session, because you asked for it. You read the whole mail first, every line is editable, and nothing is sent until you tap Send. The browser app offers the same thing on a session’s Details tab, through your own share sheet or a download and a mail. There is no CleanJibe server in either path.
TestFlight — crash reports, for as long as this is a beta
The iPhone app is installed through Apple's TestFlight, and TestFlight collects crash reports, plus the feedback you send from inside it, and passes them to us: the device model, the OS version, and the stack trace of what fell over. No session, no track, no file, nothing out of your library. That collection is Apple's, under Apple's TestFlight terms and the sharing choice you made when you accepted them — we neither add to it nor can switch it off — and it ends when the app leaves TestFlight for the App Store.
One small file on this website — is your beta build still the current one?
While this is a beta, the app fetches a single file of about three hundred bytes from cleanjibe.org — at most once a day, and nothing else about it — which says which TestFlight build is current, so it can tell you when the one you are holding is older than the one we are answering reports about. The request carries nothing: no key, no identifier, no session, not even which build is asking — it is the same static file for everyone, the comparison happens on your phone, and nothing is ever sent back. If it fails, you are told nothing and the app simply tries again tomorrow. GitHub Pages, which serves this site, sees it the way it sees any page load here: a URL, a time and an IP address. The App Store build does not do this at all.

And nothing else. The app contains no analytics, no crash reporter, no advertising, and no third-party tracking code of any kind — the crash reports above are Apple's, collected by TestFlight around the app rather than by anything we put inside it. It never asks for the App Tracking Transparency permission because it has nothing to ask for. Your data is not sold, rented or shared with anyone, and there is no one to share it with.

What the app asks permission for

Five prompts exist, all of them optional, and none of them appears until you use the feature behind it.

Health — both directions, both off by default
Writing: if you turn on workout export, CleanJibe adds your imported sessions to the Health app as workouts. Reading: if you record on an Apple Watch with Apple's own Workout app, Import → Apple Health can read those workouts — their GPS route and heart rate, and nothing else — so CleanJibe can analyse them like any other recording. It reads only the workout types you choose and only the sessions you pick, never your steps, sleep, weight or anything else in Health. Both directions are off until you switch them on, everything read stays on the phone, and nothing about a workout is ever uploaded.
Photos — add only
Asked only when you save a replay clip you just recorded to your camera roll. CleanJibe holds add-only access: it can put that one video in and cannot read your photo library. Photos you choose to splice into a clip are handed over by the system picker one at a time, without granting the app library access.
Location — one tick, one fix per map you send
There is exactly one reason CleanJibe ever asks where you are, and you have to ask for it first. Under Settings → Garmin watch → Map for the watch there is a tick called “Where I am now” — for the afternoon at a lake your library has never seen and could therefore never name. Ticking it brings up the standard iOS prompt, and what is asked for is while using the app. After that the phone reads its position once per map send: one fix, never a running update, never in the background. The coordinate is used for one thing — the corners of the 3 km box rendered on this phone and pushed to the watch. It is not stored with your sessions, not attached to anything, and not sent anywhere. Leave the tick alone and the prompt never appears at all; the automatic send at launch never prompts, and includes “Where I am now” only if you had already allowed it. Every other coordinate the app shows was already inside a file you imported.
Bluetooth — for the Garmin handoff
Used to talk to a Garmin running the CleanJibe watch app: to receive the session summary it sends when you stop recording, to send the wind direction back, and to push the kilobyte or two of coastline the watch draws under its breadcrumb. All of it rides on the Garmin Connect app's own link; CleanJibe pairs with nothing itself, and none of it goes over a network.
Notifications — only if you switch them on
Asked only when you enable the background check for new intervals.icu activities. Leave it off and the app schedules no background work.

The app never follows you around. There is one location prompt, the one above, and it exists so a map can be drawn for your watch: one fix at the moment you send a map, with the app in front of you, never in the background, and never at launch unless you had already allowed it. No background location, no geofence, no visit history, and nowhere that a position of yours is kept.

The website — cleanjibe.org

The analyzer at cleanjibe.org/app is the same engine as the phone app, running inside your browser tab.

Your file is never uploaded

When you drop a .fit file into the analyzer, it is read in the tab and analysed there. It is not sent to cleanjibe.org, and cleanjibe.org has no server that could receive it — the site is static files. Sessions you keep are stored in your own browser's storage on your own machine.

Still no account

Nothing on the site asks you to register, and there is nothing to register with. Clearing your browser's data for cleanjibe.org erases your library completely.

Your intervals.icu key, if you use one

The analyzer can hold an intervals.icu API key so it can fetch your own activities. On the web that key is kept in ordinary browser storage on your device — not in a keychain, and not as securely as on the phone. It is sent only to intervals.icu. "Forget key" removes it.

One page here is not for reading

cleanjibe.org/strava/callback exists for the iPhone app rather than for a visitor: it is where Strava returns a rider after the consent screen, and all it does is hand the result back to the app. No stylesheet, no counter, nothing third-party, nothing stored. What it is and what the server sees is under what leaves your phone.

The website — what your browser loads

A web page is a set of requests. These are all of them, and what each one can see.

umami — anonymous, cookie-free page counts
Every page here loads a small counter from Umami Cloud. It sets no cookie and stores no identifier that survives your visit, so there is nothing to consent to and nothing to opt out of. What it records is that a page was opened, which page, roughly from where, on roughly what kind of browser. It also counts named actions like a card made, with only a format, a count or a tab name. Like any web request, it necessarily reaches Umami Cloud's servers with your IP address and browser user-agent, which is how "roughly from where" is derived; Umami does not store the IP itself. It never sees your session, your file, or any number off a ride. If you would rather it did not run, any content blocker stops it and the site works identically without it.
The analysis runtime — jsDelivr and PyPI
The analyzer's engine is Python compiled to WebAssembly. The first time you analyse something, your browser downloads it — about 12 MB — from the jsDelivr CDN and one package from PyPI, then caches it for good. Those two see a download request from your IP address, as any file download does. They receive nothing about your session, which has not been read yet when they are asked.
OpenStreetMap — only for a map background you switch on
A share card and a session's own track map can both be drawn over a map. That background is off unless you turn it on, and when you do, your browser fetches map tiles for that area from the OpenStreetMap Foundation, which therefore sees the area and your IP address. Leave it off and no tile is ever requested. (The iPhone app uses Apple Maps for the same job, as above.)
OpenStreetMap — only when you look a spot name up
The browser app groups your sessions into spots and can put a name on each. That name comes from Look up names again, which you press yourself, and when you do, your browser asks the OpenStreetMap Foundation's reverse geocoder what that place is called. What is sent is one coordinate — the centre of that spot, rounded to about 110 m — and nothing else: no session, no track, no name, no identifier and no account. It is asked once per spot, at most once a second, and the answer is kept in this browser. Never press it and nothing is ever requested. (The iPhone app asks Apple's geocoder for the same thing, as above.)
intervals.icu — only if you paste a key
Same as on the phone: your key, your activities, download only.

There is nothing else. No advertising, no tracking pixels, no social widgets, no embedded fonts from anyone, no consent-management vendor, and no request that carries anything about a ride of yours.

Keeping it, and getting rid of it

There is no retention period, because there is nowhere for anything to be retained. What exists is on your device, and you control all of it.

On the phone
Swipe a session in the library to delete it — the row, the analysis and the original file all go. Clear your intervals.icu key in Settings to stop every network request the app can make. Deleting the app removes the whole library at once. One thing worth knowing: because the library is ordinary app data, an encrypted iPhone backup — iCloud or a computer — contains it, and restoring that backup brings it back. That backup is Apple's, under Apple's terms; CleanJibe cannot see it.
In the browser
Delete a session from the library, or clear site data for cleanjibe.org in your browser's settings to erase everything the analyzer has kept, key included.
Your rights, and why they are quick to exercise
Under the GDPR you have the right to access, correct, export, delete and object to the processing of your personal data. Because CleanJibe holds none of it — no account, no server, no copy of any session — there is nothing for us to hand over or erase on request, and the delete controls above are the whole of it. If you think that is wrong, write to us and we will look. You can also complain to a data protection authority in your country.

Children, changes and contact

Children
CleanJibe is a sports tool suitable for all ages and collects nothing from anyone, including children. It contains no advertising, no purchases and no way to reach another person.
Changes to this policy
If what the app or the site does with data changes, this page changes with it and the effective date at the top moves. Because this page lives in the project's public repository, every past version of it is readable there too.
Who to write to
CleanJibe is made by Jan-Rainer Lahmann in Germany. Questions about this policy, or about anything on this page you think is inaccurate, go to info@cleanjibe.org — or, if you would rather ask in public, as an issue on GitHub.

The claims on this page can be checked rather than taken on faith: the app and the site are open source at github.com/JanLahmann/WingFoil, and every network request either of them can make is in that code.
