Did you fly through that jibe?

CleanJibe reads a wingfoil session off your watch and tells you what actually happened: how much of it you spent on the foil, how long each flight lasted, your speed records, and — for every turn — whether you flew through it, touched down, or fell in.

Two ways in, and neither costs anything. The browser analyzer takes a .fit, .gpx or .tcx file and hands you the whole session back in a few seconds, with nothing to install. The beta is the pair of apps: a watch app that shows the same numbers live on your wrist, and an iPhone app that keeps every session you have ever ridden. Each one works on its own.

Get the beta Try the analyzer Free. No account. The analyzer runs in your browser — the file is never uploaded, and there is an example session inside if you have no file to hand.

Installed? Start here: your first session on the water — charge, record, save, and what you should see on the phone afterwards. There is a short dry run at the end of it if you cannot wait for wind.

The three pieces

The watch records and shows the live numbers; the phone and the browser do the full analysis afterwards. All three run the same analysis code, so a session means the same thing wherever you read it. Use any one of them on its own.

What you need, what you get

Everything reads a file, so what you get back depends on what the recording carries. Three classes, and every session lands in one of them.

Class You record with You get You do not get
a The CleanJibe watch app on a Garmin Everything: foil time, flights, every turn verdict and clean jibe, certified speed records, the wind axis, pump strokes and takeoff attempts Nothing missing
b Any other FIT with a measured speed channel — Garmin’s own Windsurf profile, another Connect IQ app Foil time, flights, turn verdicts and clean jibes, certified speed records, the wind axis Pump strokes and takeoff attempts — shown as absent, never as zero
b + wrist The CleanJibe Apple Watch app: class b speed, plus the 50 Hz wrist accelerometer Everything class b gets, and pump strokes and takeoff attempts, analysed on the phone Nothing missing; the watch itself detects nothing live
c Positions only: Strava, a .gpx, a .tcx without speed, a phone in a pocket Foil time, flights, turn verdicts and clean jibes, the wind axis, and speed records estimated from positions and marked uncertified Certified records, pump strokes, takeoff attempts

Which class a watch lands in, brand by brand, is on Which watch — including the phone-only route, which is class c and works. Take the FIT wherever an app offers you one: it carries the speed your watch measured, which is the whole difference between b and c.

Questions people actually ask

Which watches?
The CleanJibe watch app runs on 39 Garmin products — the fenix 8 and fenix 7 families, epix 2, Forerunner 255/265/955/965/570/970, MARQ 2, Enduro 3, D2, Descent Mk3, and, new in 0.9.10 and untested on real watches so far, Venu, vívoactive and Instinct 3 AMOLED. Which watch lists all of them, family by family, and says which ones nobody has ridden yet. An Apple Watch has its own CleanJibe app that records straight to the phone. Any other watch works too — what you get depends on the file, not on the brand.
Android?
There is no Android app and the analyzer does not need one: open cleanjibe.org/app in your phone browser and it works the same, library, records and trends included. Everything is stored in that browser, on that phone. What Android does not get is the iPhone app's side of things — the video export, and the map pushed to a Garmin.
Polar, Suunto, COROS — or an Apple Watch?
All of them work. Polar, Suunto and COROS sessions come in either through intervals.icu or straight out of the vendor's own app with Open in CleanJibe in the iOS share sheet; take the .fit where the app offers one, because a FIT carries the speed your watch measured and a GPX does not — that is class b rather than class c. An Apple Watch works two ways: Apple's own Workout app, read through Apple Health, or CleanJibe's watch app recording straight to the phone — and only the second one reads the wrist, so it is the Apple Watch route that gives you pump strokes and takeoff attempts. The table on Which watch has the whole of it.
No watch at all?
A phone in a waterproof pouch on your body records a perfectly good track. On an iPhone the simplest route is the Strava app — record there, then Import → Strava in CleanJibe, and no file has to move; if you would rather keep a file, Open GPX Tracker is free and shares its .gpx straight into CleanJibe or into the analyzer. On Android, record with Strava, OsmAnd or GPSLogger and drop the GPX into the analyzer in your phone browser. Start it before you launch, stop it ashore. This is class c: speed records estimated from positions and marked uncertified, and no pump strokes. Step by step on Which watch.
Strava?
Yes, on the iPhone app: Import → Strava, sign in on Strava's own consent page, and pick the sessions you want. CleanJibe only ever reads — it writes nothing back to your account — and Strava hands over positions rather than the speed your watch measured, so speed records from a Strava import are marked uncertified. Until Strava reviews the app it allows ten connected riders; if you are told the app is full, tell us. The details are on the privacy page.
What is a clean jibe?
A jibe you flew all the way through — no touchdown, no swim — and came out of still carrying your speed, with the ten seconds after it quiet too. It is the strictest of the verdicts and the one this thing is named after: flew through is keeping the foil, dry is staying out of the water, clean is both plus the speed. CPH, clean jibes per hour, sits beside JPH and WPH on every screen, and the exact thresholds are written down in the public repository rather than hidden in a product.
Where does my data go?
Nowhere. There is no CleanJibe account, no CleanJibe login and no CleanJibe server — the analysis runs on the device you are holding, in the browser tab or on the phone, and your library lives there too. The exceptions are the ones you switch on yourself (intervals.icu, Strava, a map drawn by Apple Maps) and they are all named, one by one, on the privacy page.

What it counts

The same handful of numbers on the wrist, on the phone and on this site — so a session means the same thing wherever you read it.

on the foil flew through touched down fell in

Foil %
The share of the session actually flying, rather than merely moving.
Flights & touchdowns
One takeoff starts a flight; a touchdown or a swim ends it. Both are counted.
Jibe outcomes
Every turn gets a verdict: flew through, touched down, fell in. A clean jibe is one you fly all the way through, carrying your speed.
Dry streak
How many jibes in a row you stayed out of the water — and the best run of the day.
JPH
Dry jibes per hour. Falling in more often cannot raise it.
CPH
Clean jibes per hour. The strict one, beside JPH rather than instead of it.
WPH
Swims per hour. The number nobody wants, kept honest anyway.
Speed records
GP3S-style windows: best 2 s, best 1 NM, alpha 500, best hour.
Pump & takeoff effort
Strokes counted off the accelerometer, and which attempts got you flying.

How it is built

Your file stays yours

The analyzer has no server to upload to. Your recording is read, analyzed and drawn inside your own browser tab: no account, no upload, nothing to sign up for. These pages count visits with a cookie-free page counter that never sees your session; nothing about your ride goes anywhere.

The thresholds are published

What counts as a flight, a touchdown or a fallen jibe is written down in the public repository rather than hidden in a product, so you can check the numbers against what you remember and argue with the ones that are wrong.
