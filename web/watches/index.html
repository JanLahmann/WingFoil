Which watch

The CleanJibe watch app is a Connect IQ app, so it runs on the Garmins Garmin lets it run on — 39 products as of 0.9.10, listed in full below. If yours is not on the list, the store will simply not offer the install.

Not a Garmin? That is fine, and it is the second half of this page: the analysis does not care which watch recorded the file, only what the file carries.

The three new families are untested on real watches. Venu, vívoactive and Instinct 3 AMOLED arrived in 0.9.10 and have passed the layout checks in the Connect IQ simulator at every one of their screen sizes — which proves the pages fit, and proves nothing about a session on the water. If you ride with one, that is the single most useful report this beta can get: how the pages look, and whether the touch controls behave. Where to send it.

Not on the list, and why. The Instinct 2 family and Instinct 3 Solar have 96–128 KB of app memory and cannot run the app at all; nor can the fenix 5 and fenix 6 bases. The rectangular Venu X1 is waiting for a rectangular layout rather than for a decision. There is also a CleanJibe data field on the Connect IQ store: it is parked and gets no new work — if your watch can run the full app, use the full app.

Every other watch

The analysis reads a file, not a brand. Drop a .fit, .gpx or .tcx from anything into the browser analyzer or share it into the iPhone app, and you get the track, every flight, the verdict on every turn, the wind axis and your speed records. Two things depend on the file rather than on us.

Certified speed needs a speed channel
A .fit carries the speed your watch's receiver measured, and so does a .tcx when the app that wrote it put one in. A .gpx carries positions only, so the speed has to be derived from them — which works, and is marked uncertified everywhere it appears, because an estimate and a measurement should not sit in the same column pretending to be the same thing. So: take the FIT wherever the vendor's app offers one.
Pump strokes need a wrist accelerometer
How many times you pumped, and which takeoff attempts got you flying, are read off the wrist accelerometer — and the only apps that record it are the CleanJibe watch apps. Everything else in the analysis is identical without it; the pump numbers are simply absent rather than shown as zero.

Apple Watch

Two routes, and the second one is ours. Both need the iPhone app; neither needs a Garmin anywhere.

Apple's own Workout app

Record a Surfing or Water Sports workout the way you already do, then in CleanJibe on the phone use Import → Apple Health. It reads the workout's route and heart rate and nothing else, and the speed is the watch's own, so records from it are certified. Switch on the automatic pickup and later workouts arrive by themselves.

The CleanJibe watch app

CleanJibe has its own Apple Watch app, and it came along with the TestFlight install — there is nothing else to download. It records straight to the phone, with no Health round trip and no file to move, and because it reads the wrist accelerometer it is the one Apple Watch route that gives you pump strokes and takeoff effort.

Which to use

Try Apple's Workout app first: it is what most Apple Watch riders will keep using, and it needs nothing installed on the wrist. Then try ours on a second session and tell us which one you would rather ride with — that comparison is exactly what the 20-minute test asks for.

No watch at all

A phone in a pocket records a .gpx, and a .gpx is enough for most of what this thing says: the track, the flights, the jibes and their verdicts, the wind axis. The speed records will be marked uncertified and there will be no pump data. Open the analyzer, drop the file in, and see — it costs one drag, nothing is uploaded, and there is an example session inside if you have nothing to hand.
