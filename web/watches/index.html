Which watch

The CleanJibe watch app is a Connect IQ app, so it runs on the Garmins Garmin lets it run on — 39 products as of 0.9.10, listed in full below. If yours is not on the list, the store will simply not offer the install.

Not a Garmin? That is fine, and it is the second half of this page: the analysis does not care which watch recorded the file, only what the file carries.

What you need, what you get

The analysis reads a file, so what you get back depends on what the recording carries, not on the badge on the strap. Three classes, and every session is one of them.

Every other watch

The analysis reads a file, not a brand. Drop a .fit, .gpx or .tcx from anything into the browser analyzer or share it into the iPhone app, and you get the track, every flight, the verdict on every turn, the wind axis and your speed records. Two things depend on the file rather than on us.

Certified speed needs a speed channel
A .fit carries the speed your watch's receiver measured, and so does a .tcx when the app that wrote it put one in. A .gpx carries positions only, so the speed has to be derived from them — which works, and is marked uncertified everywhere it appears, because an estimate and a measurement should not sit in the same column pretending to be the same thing. So: take the FIT wherever the vendor's app offers one.
Pump strokes need a wrist accelerometer
How many times you pumped, and which takeoff attempts got you flying, are read off the wrist accelerometer — and the only apps that record it are the CleanJibe watch apps, on a Garmin (class a) or on an Apple Watch (b + wrist, where the phone does the analysis). Everything else in the analysis is identical without it; the pump numbers are simply absent rather than shown as zero.

Apple Watch

Two routes, and the second one is ours. Both need the iPhone app; neither needs a Garmin anywhere.

Apple's own Workout appbeta

Record a Surfing or Water Sports workout the way you already do, then in CleanJibe on the phone use Import → Apple Health. It reads the workout's route and heart rate and nothing else, and the speed is the watch's own, so records from it are certified. Switch on the automatic pickup and later workouts arrive by themselves.

The CleanJibe watch appbeta

CleanJibe has its own Apple Watch app, and it came along with the TestFlight install — there is nothing else to download. It records straight to the phone, with no Health round trip and no file to move, and the speed is the watch’s own, so its records are certified. It also records the 50 Hz wrist accelerometer, which the phone reads afterwards — so it is the one Apple Watch route that gives you pump strokes and takeoff attempts (b + wrist). Apple’s own Workout app does not.

Which to use

Try Apple's Workout app first: it is what most Apple Watch riders will keep using, and it needs nothing installed on the wrist. Then try ours on a second session and tell us which one you would rather ride with — that comparison is exactly what the start guide asks for.

No watch at all

A phone in a waterproof pouch records a .gpx, and a .gpx is enough for most of what this thing says: the track, the flights, the jibes and their verdicts, the wind axis. This is class c — the speed records are estimated from positions and marked uncertified, and there are no pump strokes. Nothing else is missing.

iPhone

Apple’s own Fitness and Workout apps need an Apple Watch, so on a phone alone they are not the answer. Three that are:

  • Strava — the simplest, because no file has to move. Record with Record in the Strava app, then in CleanJibe use Import → Strava and pick the session. (Strava’s own file export is on strava.com in a browser, not in the app — which is exactly why the direct connection is the route.)
  • Open GPX Tracker — free, open source, no account. Record, then share the .gpx and choose Open in CleanJibe, or drop it into the analyzer.
  • Komoot — record a tour, then Download GPX file from the completed activity. The region you start in has to be unlocked; one is free.

Android

There is no CleanJibe app for Android and none is needed: open cleanjibe.org/app in the phone browser and drop the file in.

Install it, and it joins the share sheet. In Chrome, open cleanjibe.org/app and take Add to Home screen (or Install app) from the menu — or press Install on the bar the page offers you. It gets an icon, opens without browser chrome and works with no signal. After that, hold a .fit or .gpx anywhere on the phone — Files, Gmail, Drive, your watch's own app — and choose Share → CleanJibe: the analysis opens on it, in the browser, with the file still on the phone.

  • Strava — record in the app; the export is on strava.com, so open the activity there and take Export GPX.
  • OsmAnd — Trip recording, then My Places → Tracks → Share, which hands over a .gpx.
  • GPSLogger — a plain, tiny logger that writes GPX and shares it. It is on F-Droid rather than in the Play Store.
  • Komoot — as on the iPhone; the GPX lands in Downloads.

However you record it

  • Phone in a waterproof pouch on your body — on the board it is a swim away from being gone, and in a bag on the beach it records the beach.
  • Start it before you launch and let it run; stop it once you are ashore. The analysis finds the riding inside a recording that has standing about at both ends.
  • Then export the file and share it into CleanJibe — or connect Strava and skip the file altogether.
  • Expect class c: no pumping, and speed records marked uncertified. A phone on your body is also a noisier receiver than a watch, so treat the top speed as a story rather than a record.

No file to hand at all? The analyzer has an example session one click inside — it costs one drag, and nothing is uploaded.
