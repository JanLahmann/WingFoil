The three pieces

The watch records and shows the live numbers; the phone and the browser do the full analysis afterwards. All three run the same analysis code, so a session means the same thing wherever you read it. Use any one of them on its own.

CleanJibe for Garmin

open beta · Connect IQ

Connect IQ watch app — 39 watches, fenix 8 down to vívoactive

  • Knows when the board is up on the foil, and counts every flight and touchdown as you ride.
  • Scores each jibe as flew through, touched down or fell in — and keeps your dry streak running.
  • Clean jibes and CPH on the wrist, with their own buzz, beside the tally.
  • Speed records live: best 2 s and best 10 s, with a PB alert.
  • Works out the wind direction by itself after a few minutes of riding.
  • Counts pump strokes and takeoff attempts from the wrist accelerometer.
  • Records as a Windsurf activity, so it no longer lands in Garmin Connect as a walk.

Questions people actually ask

Which watches?
The CleanJibe watch app runs on 39 Garmin products — the fenix 8 and fenix 7 families, epix 2, Forerunner 255/265/955/965/570/970, MARQ 2, Enduro 3, D2, Descent Mk3, and, untested on real watches so far, Venu, vívoactive, Instinct 3 AMOLED and the fenix 5 Plus family. Which watch lists all of them, family by family, and says which ones nobody has ridden yet. An Apple Watch has its own CleanJibe app that records straight to the phone. Any other watch works too — what you get depends on the file, not on the brand.
Android?
There is no Android app and the analyzer does not need one: open cleanjibe.org/app in your phone browser and it works the same, library, records and trends included. Install it from Chrome's menu — Add to Home screen / Install app — and it gets an icon, it opens without browser chrome, it works with no signal, and it joins Android's share sheet: hold a .fit or .gpx in Files, Gmail, Drive or your watch's own app, choose Share → CleanJibe, and the analysis opens on it. Everything is stored in that browser, on that phone. What Android does not get is the iPhone app's side of things — the video export, and the map pushed to a Garmin.
Polar, Suunto, COROS — or an Apple Watch?
All of them work. Polar, Suunto and COROS sessions come in either through intervals.icu or straight out of the vendor's own app with Open in CleanJibe in the iOS share sheet; take the .fit where the app offers one, because a FIT carries the speed your watch measured and a GPX does not — that is class b rather than class c. An Apple Watch works two ways: Apple's own Workout app, read through Apple Health, or CleanJibe's watch app recording straight to the phonebeta — and only the second one reads the wrist, so it is the Apple Watch route that gives you pump strokes and takeoff attempts. The table on Which watch has the whole of it.
No watch at all?
A phone in a waterproof pouch on your body records a perfectly good track. On an iPhone the simplest route is the Strava app — record there, then Import → Strava in CleanJibe, and no file has to move; if you would rather keep a file, Open GPX Tracker is free and shares its .gpx straight into CleanJibe or into the analyzer. On Android, record with Strava, OsmAnd or GPSLogger, install the analyzer from Chrome's menu, and share the GPX straight into it. Start it before you launch, stop it ashore. This is class c: speed records estimated from positions and marked uncertified, and no pump strokes. Step by step on Which watch.
Strava?
Yes, on the iPhone app: Import → Strava, sign in on Strava's own consent page, and pick the sessions you want. CleanJibe only ever reads — it writes nothing back to your account — and Strava hands over positions rather than the speed your watch measured, so speed records from a Strava import are marked uncertified. Strava lets a new app connect a limited number of riders; if you are told the app is full, tell us. The details are on the privacy page.
What is a clean jibe?
A jibe you flew all the way through — no touchdown, no swim — and came out of still carrying your speed, with the ten seconds after it quiet too. It is the strictest of the verdicts and the one this thing is named after: flew through is keeping the foil, dry is staying out of the water, clean is both plus the speed. CPH, clean jibes per hour, sits beside JPH and WPH on every screen, and the exact thresholds are written down in the public repository rather than hidden in a product.
Where does my data go?
Nowhere. There is no CleanJibe account, no CleanJibe login and no CleanJibe server — the analysis runs on the device you are holding, in the browser tab or on the phone, and your library lives there too. The exceptions are the ones you switch on yourself (intervals.icu, Strava, a map drawn by Apple Maps) and they are all named, one by one, on the privacy page.

What it counts

The same handful of numbers on the wrist, on the phone and on this site — so a session means the same thing wherever you read it.

on the foil flew through touched down fell in

Foil %
How much of the session was spent flying rather than merely moving.
Flights & touchdowns
One takeoff starts a flight; a touchdown or a swim ends it. Both are counted.
Turn verdicts
Every turn gets one: flew through, touched down, or fell in.
Dry streak
How many jibes in a row you stayed out of the water — and the best run of the day.
JPH
Dry jibes per hour. Falling in more often cannot raise it.
CPH
Clean jibes per hour: you flew through it, you held your speed, and the ten seconds after it stayed quiet.
WPH
Swims per hour. The number nobody wants, kept honest anyway.
Speed records
Your fastest 2 seconds, 10 seconds, 500 m and nautical mile — the same windows the speedsurfing world uses.
Pump & takeoff effort
Strokes counted off the accelerometer, and which attempts got you flying.

How it is built

Your file stays yours

The analyzer has no server to upload to. Your recording is read, analyzed and drawn inside your own browser tab: no account, no upload, nothing to sign up for. These pages count visits with a cookie-free page counter that never sees your session; nothing about your ride goes anywhere.

The thresholds are published

What counts as a flight, a touchdown or a fallen jibe is written down in the public repository rather than hidden in a product, so you can check the numbers against what you remember and argue with the ones that are wrong.

Tell us. Bugs, verdicts you disagree with, and — just as much — what you would want this to do next. Ideas and wishes are as welcome as bugs. In the app, Menu → Support & ideas is the best door, because it prefills the facts; otherwise mail info@cleanjibe.org or open a GitHub issue. Need something that is only in the beta? Tell us.
