One session on the water

Get started with CleanJibe

The test is one ordinary session. Ride the way you always do, then open it on the phone and read the verdicts against what you remember — that jibe you touched down in, the one you flew through and knew it. Nobody but you can check that, which is the whole reason this thing exists.

The walkthrough is below: charge, record, save, sync, read, share a card, tell us. Then what you should see, and where to put it.

No wind this week? There is a 20-minute check on land further down — it proves the recording reaches the phone, which is the half of the route that goes wrong.

Before you start

Four things, and the last one is the one people skip.

  1. Install the iPhone app

    CleanJibe Wingfoil Analyzer is free on the App Store: apps.apple.com/app/id6800401377. Install it and open it once — the app never asks for your position at all.

    Routes B, C and D below need the beta instead — the Apple Watch app, Apple Health and the GPX/TCX imports are the doors still proving themselves. Open testflight.apple.com/join/nygqGGcn on the iPhone, install Apple's TestFlight app from the App Store if it asks, and CleanJibe installs through it in place of the App Store app, library and all. Allow Health when it asks — that is the only permission the beta adds. If TestFlight later offers an update, take it before you start: it is worth being on the newest build for a test whose whole point is telling us what broke.

  2. Garmin riders: install the watch app

    CleanJibe Wingfoil Tracker (Beta) on the Connect IQ store — from the web store, or from Connect IQ inside the Garmin Connect app on your phone. It is an open beta: no key, no account, nothing to request.

  3. Watch on the wrist, unlocked, paired

    Both watch routes want the watch you actually wear, paired with the iPhone you installed CleanJibe on.

  4. Go outside

    Every route needs GPS, and a watch indoors records no positions at all — which produces exactly the empty session that looks like a bug and is not one.

On the water — the real test

One normal session. The only extra work is the last two steps, and they are the ones we cannot do without.

  1. Charge the watch, and the phone

    A GPS activity with the screen on is the most expensive thing a watch does, and a session that stops at 70 % battery is a session nobody can read a verdict out of. Top both up the evening before.

  2. Start CleanJibe on the watch, on the beach

    Press START, pick CleanJibe from the activity list, and wait until it says GPS good before you press START again. Do it while you are rigging: a fix takes longer than you think and the first minutes of a session are the ones you are least willing to spend standing still.

  3. Ride, and glance at the wrist once

    Ride the session you came for. Somewhere in the middle, look at the tally — flew, touched down, fell in — and the dry streak, and see whether they match the last ten minutes. That one glance is worth more than a paragraph written afterwards.

  4. Stop and save

    STOP, then Save. Flick through the summary pages once (UP/DOWN) before you put the watch away and note anything that looks wrong.

  5. Let Garmin Connect sync

    The watch uploads to Garmin Connect by itself next to the phone — open Garmin Connect once if it is taking its time. From there intervals.icu brings it to CleanJibe; that bridge is set up once and never again.

    Apple Watch, Polar, Suunto, COROS or a phone: your recording gets in a different way, and the routes are the next section down. Everything after this step is the same.

  6. Pull down on Sessions

    Open CleanJibe on the iPhone and pull down on the Sessions list. That is the refresh: it checks intervals.icu, imports what is new, and analyses it as it lands. Your session appears at the top with the day’s numbers on it.

  7. Open it, and check the verdicts against your memory

    This is the test. Open the session, go to Turns, and read down the list: every turn has a verdict — flew through, touched down, fell in — and the clean jibes are marked among them. Tap one and the page says why it was scored that way.

    Where does it disagree with you? A jibe you know you flew through that is marked as a touchdown, a swim it never noticed, a wind direction that is not the one you sailed in. Those three sentences are the most useful report this project can get.

  8. Share a card

    Share button, top right → share card: your track, your numbers, one picture. Send it to whoever you were on the water with. It is also how anyone else finds out this exists, so tell us if anything on it reads wrong or looks cheap.

  9. Send the report: Menu → Support

    Top left on the Sessions tab: Menu → Support. The build, your phone, your iOS version, your watch and the size of your library are already typed in; you add what the session got right and what it got wrong, and press Send. The other doors are at the bottom of this page.

The minimal check, if you cannot wait

Twenty minutes, no water, and it answers exactly one question: does a recording made on your watch reach CleanJibe on your phone? That is the half of the route with three apps in it and the half that goes wrong. It proves nothing about the analysis — a bike ride has no jibes to judge — so it is a check before the session, not a substitute for it.

Three to five minutes of movement is plenty. A bike ride gives the nicest result — CleanJibe calls anything above about 12 km/h “flying”, so a ride draws a track with real flights in it. Walking works too. Four routes, and you only need the one that matches what is on your wrist — they are also how a recording gets in from each kind of watch on a real session.

beta in the public beta today, not yet in the App Store release.

Route A · Garmin

fenix, epix, Forerunner and the rest

The Garmin app does the analysis on the wrist; the iPhone app reads the recording afterwards, over intervals.icu.

  1. Record and save

    On the watch press START, pick CleanJibe from the activity list, and wait until it says GPS good. Press START, move for three to five minutes, then STOP and Save.

    Flick through the summary pages once (UP/DOWN) and note anything that looks wrong — that is half the test on this route.

  2. Let the watch sync

    It uploads to Garmin Connect by itself next to the phone.

  3. Bridge it with intervals.icu

    All on the phone: make a free account at intervals.icu, and in its Settings connect Garmin Connect, so your activities flow in by themselves. Then in Settings → Developer settings copy your Athlete ID and create a Personal API key.

    In CleanJibe on the iPhone: Settings → intervals.icu, paste both, and use the check button. Then + → Sync intervals.icu. The session appears in the library.

  4. No intervals.icu? The long way, with a computer

    connect.garmin.com → the activity → the gear icon → Export File (it read Export Original until Garmin renamed it). AirDrop the file to the iPhone, then + → FIT, GPX or ZIP in CleanJibe. Garmin has no export on the phone at all, which is why intervals.icu is the route above.

Route B · the CleanJibe watch appbeta

Apple Watch · second

Our own recorder on the watch. It comes with the beta from TestFlight — there is nothing else to download, and it is not in the App Store app.

  1. Put it on the watch

    On the iPhone, open Apple’s Watch app, scroll down to Available apps, and install CleanJibe if it is not on the watch yet.

  2. Record

    Open CleanJibe on the watch and allow Health (heart rate) and Location if it asks. Wait until the screen no longer says NO GPS, press START, and move for three to five minutes.

    Press Stop. The summary says Saved on the watch; press Done.

  3. Let it arrive

    Open CleanJibe on the iPhone. The session comes across on its own while phone and watch are together. If the watch still says 1 session waiting for iPhone, leave the iPhone app in front of you for a minute.

Route C · Apple’s own Workout appbeta

Apple Watch · do this first

Nothing to install on the watch. This is how most Apple Watch riders will use CleanJibe.

  1. Start a Surfing workout

    On the watch, open Workout and pick Surfing (scroll or search; Water Sports works too). Not Sailing, not Walking.

  2. Move for three to five minutes

    Wait a few seconds for the GPS, start it, and go. Then end the workout on the watch (swipe right, End) and let it save.

  3. Import it on the iPhone

    Open CleanJibe, tap the + import button, and choose Import from Health. The first time, allow CleanJibe to read workouts and routes in Health — every switch on, or there is nothing to read.

    Your workout is listed as Surfing · 4 min · today. Tap it, or Import all.

Route D · Polar, Suunto, COROS — or anything else

no CleanJibe app on the wrist

Record the way you already do, then hand the file to CleanJibe. Nothing to install on the watch, because there is nothing for us to install there.

  1. Record and save on your own watch

    Any outdoor activity with GPS. Three to five minutes, the same as the other routes.

  2. Share the file into CleanJibe

    In the vendor’s phone app, export the workout and pick Open in CleanJibe from the iOS share sheet. Suunto: Calendar → the workout → ⋯ → FIT. COROS: Activities → the activity → ⋯ → Export → FIT. Polar has no export on the phone at all — use flow.polar.com on a computer and AirDrop the file over.

    Take the FIT where the app offers one: a FIT carries the speed your watch measured, a .gpx does not, and a .tcx only does when the app wrote one in. Everything else — foil time, flights, turns, the map, the wind axis — is identical either way. A FIT opens in the App Store app; .gpx and .tcx need the beta.beta

  3. Or let intervals.icu do it

    Most of these watches sync to intervals.icu too, and from there CleanJibe picks them up by itself — Settings → intervals.icu, then + → Sync intervals.icu, exactly as in route A.

Which brand gets which numbers is one table, on Which watch.

Bonus: the share card and the video

Optional, two minutes, and the part we can least test on our own.

The share card

On the session page, tap the share button (top right). The share card is the picture with your track and your numbers on it. Send it to us the way you would send it to a friend, and say whether it looks good and whether anything on it is confusing.

Export video

In the same sheet: keep the 20 s preset, wait for the bar, then share the clip. Did the map draw itself smoothly? Did the end card look right? This one is new and has only ever been watched on a simulator.

What you should see

Open the session in CleanJibe on the iPhone. Three things say the route worked — after a session on the water you get the turn verdicts as well, which is the part only you can check.

A map with your track on it
Teal where you were “flying” (fast), grey where you were not.
Duration, distance and speeds at the top
Numbers that match roughly what you actually did.
A speed chart under the map
It moves when you drag along it.
Turns and jibes reading zero
Expected on a bike or on foot: there is nothing to jibe. You are testing the watch route here, not the wingfoil analysis.

Your old sessions

Once one test session has arrived, the interesting part is your real history. Everything CleanJibe imports is analysed as it lands: open a session and swipe through Ride, Turns, Takeoffs and Log, and Records and Trends fill up by themselves.

Apple Watch

+ → Import from Health lists every Surfing and Water Sports workout of the last two years. Tap Import all. Switch on the automatic pickup on that screen and new workouts arrive on their own from then on.

Recorded old sessions under another type — Sailing, say? Which workouts to offer, on the same screen, adds it.

Garmin

intervals.icu brings in what Garmin sends it from the day you connect the two, and Sync intervals.icu takes everything it finds there — so recent sessions come this way.

For the years before: in Garmin Connect on the web, Account → Export Your Data. Garmin mails you a ZIP; open it on the iPhone in CleanJibe (+ → Garmin export ZIP) and it imports every wingfoil and windsurf session inside at once. A few hundred sessions take a few minutes.

Strava

+ → Strava signs you in on Strava’s own consent page — read-only; CleanJibe writes nothing back — and lists your matching activities of the last two years, with the ones already in your library marked. Tap the ones you want, or Import all new. The one door that needs no Garmin account and no intervals.icu.

Strava hands over positions rather than the speed your watch measured, so those sessions’ speed records are marked uncertified. If the same afternoon is on intervals.icu, take it from there instead. And until Strava has reviewed the app it allows ten connected riders, which the app says plainly; if you are told the app is full, tell us.

Anything else

+ → FIT, GPX or ZIP, or Open in CleanJibe from the iOS share sheet in any app that can export a workout — .fit, .gpx and .tcx all come in. That is the route for a Polar, a Suunto or a COROS, and the table on Which watch says what each format gives you.

If something does not work

Try the line that matches, then tell us either way — a fix that took you three attempts is still a report worth having.

“Nothing to import” (route C)
The workout is not in Health yet, or CleanJibe is not allowed to read it. iPhone Settings → Health → Data Access & Devices → CleanJibe, everything on. Then try again, and check Which workouts to offer for the type you used.
The watch says NO GPS for more than a minute (route B)
Open sky, wrist still, wait. A balcony under a roof is not open sky.
The session never reaches the iPhone (route B)
Open CleanJibe on the iPhone and leave it in front for a minute, Bluetooth on, watch next to it.
CleanJibe is not offered in the Watch app
Reinstall the CleanJibe beta from TestFlight — the watch app is in the beta only — then look again under Available apps.
Garmin: CleanJibe is not in the activity list
Open Garmin Connect on the phone, let it sync, look again. The install can take a minute to reach the watch.
Garmin: nothing arrives from intervals.icu
Check the activity is visible on intervals.icu first — the Garmin link can take a few minutes — then Sync again.
Garmin: the ZIP will not open in CleanJibe
Unzip it on the computer and AirDrop the .fit file inside instead.

Whatever happens, it is useful. A test that fails tells us more than one that works.

Tell us what you saw

One short message per route. A screenshot of the session page is worth more than a paragraph, and “this number looks wrong” is a perfectly good report.

From inside the app — Menu → Support

The easiest door, and the one that arrives complete. Top left on the Sessions tab: Menu → Support. The same mail is under Settings → Send feedback, behind Something off? Send feedback at the foot of every page, and behind Report a problem with this session on a session’s share sheet — that last one names the session and attaches its card. Your build, your phone, your iOS version, your watch and the size of your library are already typed in; you add what happened and press Send. Nothing leaves the phone until you do.

Mail — the template is prefilled

info@cleanjibe.org — the link opens a mail with the fields already in it. Attach the screenshot and send.

TestFlight’s own feedbackbeta

If you are on the beta: in the TestFlight app, CleanJibe → Send Beta Feedback. Or take a screenshot anywhere inside CleanJibe and choose Share Beta Feedback. Either way it reaches us with your device, your iOS version and the exact build attached — nothing for you to look up. From the App Store app, Menu → Support is the door that does the same job.

Garmin, from the store listing

On the CleanJibe listing: Contact Developer for a message and Report a Problem for a bug, both of which land with the same person. A store review helps other riders find it at all.

What to put in it:

Which route
A (Workout app), B (CleanJibe watch app) or C (Garmin).
Your watch
Apple: model and watchOS version (Settings → General → About). Garmin: model and software version (Settings → System → About).
Did it arrive
Yes or no, and how long it took.
A screenshot
The session page. Was the track drawn? Were parts of it teal?
Anything odd
A permission you were never asked for, a “Nothing to import” screen, a NO GPS that never cleared, a crash, wording you did not understand.

Something bigger than a test report — a feature, a number you disagree with, a session read wrong — is best as a GitHub issue, where the next rider with the same question can read the answer. Attach the .fit file if a session was read wrong: with the file the detection can be re-run and fixed, without it there is only guesswork.

Tell us. Bugs, verdicts you disagree with, and — just as much — ideas and wishes: what you would want this to do next. In the app, Menu → Support is the best door, because it prefills the facts; otherwise mail info@cleanjibe.org or open a GitHub issue. Need something that is only in the beta? Tell us.
